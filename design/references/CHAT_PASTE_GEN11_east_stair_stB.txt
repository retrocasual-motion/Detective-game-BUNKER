Reference photograph, autumn 2009, inside a modern privately built underground shelter in Colorado, made inside an old cold-war missile site; nobody there; state before any incident. The east service stair is a narrow vertical stairwell shaft of monolithic concrete, about 3 m wide and 6 m long, about 4 m deep from the upper floor to the lower floor. At the top, at one short end of the shaft, a small landing about 1.5 by 2 m of diamond-pattern steel tread plate sticks out over the shaft; its open edge drops straight down about 4 m to a concrete floor pad at the bottom. From the landing one steep straight steel stair flight, about 40 to 45 degrees and about 6 m long, runs down along one long wall of the shaft to the bottom pad; at the bottom a plain steel door. Guardrail: along the open edge of the landing and along the open side of the flight, painted steel posts with a top rail at about 1.07 m and a mid rail; where the landing rail turns into the flight rail there is a corner post standing on a small old dark cast-iron base plate bolted to the landing floor, plain and unremarkable. Walls: bare concrete, the lower band painted muted olive. Used every day for months: worn paint on the top rail where hands slide, scuffed tread edges, shoe marks on the landing plate, small chips in the paint, a little dust. Not dirty, not ruined. Soft light: lamps in wire cage guards on the shaft walls give warm-white pools of light, gentle shadows between them; calm, slightly moody, not flat office light, not dark. Natural colours, slight grain, everything in focus.
